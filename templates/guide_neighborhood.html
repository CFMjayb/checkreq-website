What this page is

A picture of the neighborhood around your church: how many people and households live there, their ages, incomes, languages, housing, schools and more. It uses public Census data, not your parish's own records, so it describes the neighbors, not your congregation.

The 7-minute and 15-minute drive

The area is everything you can reach by car from the church in 7 minutes, or in 15. Use the two buttons above the map to switch. The shaded shapes on the map are those areas. They follow the roads, so they are not circles. The gold dots are other parishes known to Beacon that sit inside the areas.

The comparisons

Most cards show the same number for your county, your state and the U.S., so you can see where your neighborhood differs. The county is the one the church is in.

“Use caution” and “unreliable”

The Census asks a sample of households, so every number has a margin of error. In a small area, especially in the country, it can be large. When it is, the number carries a flag: use caution means the number could be off by a lot, unreliable means treat it as a rough hint at most. The number is still shown, so you can decide for yourself.

How current is it?

The footer says which Census release the numbers come from and when Beacon built them. Most of it is the American Community Survey 2020–2024, which is an average over five years, not a count of one year. The unemployment rate here, for example, is that five-year average, not this month's rate. Generations use ages as of 2022.

Three kinds of numbers that are not survey counts

  • Growth compares the 2000, 2010 and 2020 Census counts of the people and households inside the area, as a rate per year. A very small starting count swings the rate a lot, so it carries a flag.
  • Daytime population is the jobs located in the area plus the residents who do not leave for work elsewhere. It uses the Census jobs data (2023) with the 2020 Census count of residents. It shows how busy the area is on a workday, not who lives there.
  • Schools nearby lists the schools nearest the church inside the area, from the National Center for Education Statistics. Public schools are complete; the private-school file is a sample, so a private school may be missing.
  • Health checkups and dental visits are model-based estimates from the CDC (PLACES), not counts. They are shown only when the CDC has numbers for almost all of the area's adults, so some areas show none.

If the page says it is not available

The page appears once the diocesan office has confirmed where your church is and the numbers have been built for your diocese. If you see a message instead of numbers, ask the diocesan office to check your church's location. If your church is not on the map at all, nothing is wrong on your side.
